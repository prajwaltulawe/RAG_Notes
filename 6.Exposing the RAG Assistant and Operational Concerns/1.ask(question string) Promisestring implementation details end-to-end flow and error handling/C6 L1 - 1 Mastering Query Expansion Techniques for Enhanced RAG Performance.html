Productionizing RAGs: Query Normalization / Rewrite
With breakthroughs in LLMs, Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG)-based systems have gained great popularity among industry leaders. However, productionizing a RAG system comes with its own challenges and pain points.
If you haven’t already, please check out my earlier article: Productionizing RAGs: Challenges, for an overview of the challenges.
This article focuses on one of the pain points: augmenting queries.
Regardless of the quality of the knowledge base, technology, or algorithm, a RAG system can’t produce good results if it can’t understand the user query. As a result, methodologies have been developed to modify user queries to make them more understandable for the models:
Acronym Expansion
Original query: “How do I do my bf?”
Interpretation #1: “How do I do my boy friend?”
Interpretation #2: “How do I do my breakfast?”
LLMs can easily make the first interpretation when the intended one is the second. This ambiguity can lead LLMs to produce funny, irrelevant, or unacceptable responses.
Augmenting queries by expanding acronyms is one of the most straightforward ways to augment queries, though it can be complex depending on the context. In its simplest form, a basic key-value lookup should suffice. However, depending on the context, more complex systems might need to be designed.
Synonym Expansion
This is similar to acronym expansion. However, instead of abbreviations, words or phrases in a query that are repetitive or could be expressed more vividly are replaced.
Original Query: I would like to buy a cheap t-shirt.
Expanded Query: I would like to buy an affordable or inexpensive t-shirt.
In the example above, replacing ‘cheap’ with ‘affordable’ helps the RAG system retrieve more relevant information, such as suggestions for buying inexpensive t-shirts.
Contextual Query Expansion
This method improves the quality of RAG systems by adding contextual information that helps retrieve relevant documents. This approach uses vector embeddings, knowledge graphs, LLMs to reformulate queries, or terms from top-ranked retrievals to include contextually relevant information in the query.
For example,
Original Query: climate change
Expanded Query: [impact of climate change on local economy, what are some factors contributing to climate change]
However, query expansion has limitations, as the quality of the expansion is limited by the sources used to expand the queries. Additionally, using terms from top-ranked retrievals usually requires extra ranking criteria. This approach may reduce precision but can improve recall.
Knowledge Graphs
Consider a conversational system designed to answer health-related questions.
A user inputs the query, “What are the risks of heart disease in athletes?”
A knowledge graph might establish relationships between “cardiovascular risks” and “heart diseases.” Similarly, relationships can be drawn between “athletes” and “endurance sports” or “over-training.” Based on this information, the user query can be rewritten as:
“What are the cardiovascular risks associated with endurance sports or over-training in athletes?”
The goal of query augmentation is to eliminate ambiguities in user inputs to retrieve relevant candidates for context. Knowledge graphs (KG) help achieve this by establishing relationships.
However, the main drawback of this approach is its complexity and data requirements.
Neural Query Rewriting
This approach leverages deep learning networks to refine, augment, and/or rewrite user inputs to add relevance and remove ambiguities. It’s particularly effective in systems where user queries are ambiguous, incomplete, or imprecise, as it applies learned patterns and contextual understanding to dynamically improve queries. The system can be designed to learn from user feedback and adapt to evolving user needs and intents.
Any LLM can be fine-tuned for this approach. However, the major challenge is the need for large volumes of high-quality query-response pairs for training. Hallucinations and bias are other issues associated with using Neural Query Rewriting (NQR) for query augmentation.
