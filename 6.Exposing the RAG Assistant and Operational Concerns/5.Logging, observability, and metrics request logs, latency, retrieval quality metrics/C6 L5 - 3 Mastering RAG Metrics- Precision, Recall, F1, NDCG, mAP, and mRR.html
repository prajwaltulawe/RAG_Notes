Tips to Understand RAG Retrieval Metrics
When evaluating a RAG system, understanding the metrics used to assess the effectiveness of retrieval systems is crucial. This guide dives into several key retrieval metrics, offering tips on how to interpret them effectively.
Before reading this guide, if you’re not familiar with each metric, I recommend checking out this excellent article.
1. Retrieval Precision
Definition
Retrieval precision is the ratio of relevant documents retrieved to the total number of documents retrieved. It indicates the accuracy of the retrieval system.
Tips for Reading Retrieval Precision
•
Importance with Short Context Length: Precision is especially critical when working with language models (LLMs) that have limited context length. High precision ensures the limited space is used effectively by including only the most relevant documents.
•
Reducing Hallucination: When aiming to minimize irrelevant or misleading information (hallucinations), focusing on precision is beneficial. High precision means fewer irrelevant documents, which reduces the risk of hallucinations.
2. Retrieval Recall
Definition
Retrieval recall is the ratio of relevant documents retrieved to the total number of relevant documents in the dataset. It measures the system’s ability to find all relevant documents.
Tips for Reading Retrieval Recall
•
General Usefulness: Recall is generally useful across a wide range of scenarios. High recall ensures the retrieval system doesn’t miss relevant documents.
•
Understanding Recall Values: A recall of 0.5 means the retrieval system is only retrieving 50% of the relevant documents. It’s a clear indicator of how much information the retrieval process might be leaving out.
3. Retrieval F1 Score
Definition
The F1 score is the harmonic mean of precision and recall. It balances the two metrics, providing a single score that accounts for both false positives and false negatives.
Tips for Reading Retrieval F1 Score
•
When Not to Use: Avoid the F1 score if you’re specifically trying to maximize either precision or recall on their own. It’s most useful when you need a balance between the two.
•
Harmonic Mean: The F1 score is handy when you want a single metric that reflects both precision and recall performance of the retrieval system.
4. Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG)
Definition
NDCG is a measure of ranking quality that takes into account the position of relevant documents in the result list. It assigns higher scores to relevant documents that appear higher up in the list.
Tips for Reading NDCG
•
Rank-Aware Metric: NDCG considers document ranking, making it ideal for evaluating the quality of the ranking produced by the retrieval system.
•
Complexity: While it may be more complex to grasp than simpler metrics like precision and recall, NDCG effectively evaluates retrieval module performance, especially when result order matters.
5. Mean Average Precision (mAP)
Definition
Mean Average Precision (mAP) is the mean of the average precision scores across all queries. It considers both precision and recall at various recall levels.
Tips for Reading mAP
•
Rank-Aware Metric: Like NDCG, mAP is rank-aware, making it great for evaluating systems where retrieval order is important.
•
Interpretation: An mAP of 0.2 means that, on average, the system places correct answers within the top 5 retrieved passages. It’s straightforward to understand.
6. Mean Reciprocal Rank (mRR)
Definition
Mean Reciprocal Rank (mRR) is the average of the reciprocal ranks of the first relevant document for each query.
Tips for Reading mRR
•
Multiple Passages: mRR shines when handling multiple passages with similar meaning. It focuses on the position of the first relevant document, clearly showing how well the retrieval system ranks relevant items at the top.
By understanding these metrics and their use cases, you can better evaluate and optimize your retrieval systems. Each has its strengths and ideal scenarios, so choose based on your specific needs and goals.
