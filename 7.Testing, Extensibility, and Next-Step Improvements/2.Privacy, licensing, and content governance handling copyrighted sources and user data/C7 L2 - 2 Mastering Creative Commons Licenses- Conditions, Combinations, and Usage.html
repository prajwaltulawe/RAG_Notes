Creative Commons License
Placing our works under a Creative Commons license doesn’t mean they lack copyright. This type of license grants certain rights to third parties under specific conditions. What conditions?
Attribution (Reconocimiento): Any authorized use of the work under the license requires acknowledging the authorship.
Non-Commercial (No Comercial): Use of the work is limited to non-commercial purposes.
No Derivative Works (Sin obras derivadas): Authorization to use the work does not include transforming it to create derivative works.
Share Alike (Compartir Igual): Authorized use includes creating derivative works, provided they are released under the same license.
These conditions can be combined to create the six Creative Commons licenses:
Attribution (by): Any use of the work is permitted, including commercial purposes, as well as creating derivative works, which can also be distributed without any restrictions.
Attribution - Non-Commercial (by-nc): Creating derivative works is allowed as long as they are not used commercially. The original work also cannot be used for commercial purposes.
Attribution - Non-Commercial - Share Alike (by-nc-sa): Commercial use of the original work or any derivative works is not permitted. Distribution of derivatives must be under the same license as the original work.
Attribution - Non-Commercial - No Derivatives (by-nc-nd): Commercial use of the original work or creation of derivative works is not permitted.
Attribution - Share Alike (by-sa): Commercial use of the work and any derivative works is permitted, but distribution of derivatives must be under the same license as the original work.
Attribution - No Derivatives (by-nd): Commercial use of the work is permitted, but creating derivative works is not.
Obtaining the License
Once you’ve made your choice, you’ll get the appropriate license for your work expressed in three ways:
•
Commons Deed: A easily understandable summary of the legal text with the relevant icons.
•
Legal Code: The full legal code on which the license you’ve chosen is based.
•
Digital Code: The machine-readable digital code that search engines and other applications use to identify your work and its usage conditions.
Using the License
Once you’ve chosen the license, you need to include the Creative Commons “Some Rights Reserved” button on your site, near your work. This button links to the Commons Deed, so everyone can be informed of the license conditions. If you find that your license has been violated, you’ll have the basis to defend your rights.
