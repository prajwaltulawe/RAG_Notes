Cracking the AI Code: How Vector Databases Are Revolutionizing Our Digital World
Like any technology, Artificial Intelligence has a complex system architecture behind it that needs to be built as efficiently as possible.
So, what does “efficient” mean in AI?
Well, efficient AI means it’s cheap, responds quickly, and provides coherent and accurate answers. This phrase may seem simple, but it’s not; behind it, in that architecture, you have to orchestrate several things.
We’re going to focus first on vector databases and the 4 prior steps involved.
We’ll zero in on a product that’s increasingly coming into the spotlight: RAG. RAG is a pattern for storing data so you can later ask questions and the system returns an accurate response quickly from a body of information. In other words, you store information in a system and ask questions conversationally.
To do this, imagine storing 3 types of information: the web content of our business, text documents (PDF, Word, txt…), and plain text in a txt file with correct and incorrect questions and answers so the intelligent system knows how to respond one way or another. That is, we teach it what’s right and what’s wrong (according to our criteria, of course).
In the four circles shown in the image, there are 4 types of software we need to use for data processing. Explained in a casual and simple way:
1.
Scan the document.
2.
Chunk its content.
3.
Convert it into numbers in matrices.
4.
Store it in the database (Vectorial)
In other words, in AI, documents aren’t just “uploaded” or stored in the system like in a traditional database—no. They’re stored differently, going through prior steps and being converted into numerical matrices. So why not do it like a traditional database?
You might wonder. Because when someone asks an AI a question, the AI has to search for that information in the database very, very quickly to give a fast response in turn. And the best way to do that right now is to search in numerical matrices. That’s why we need powerful graphics cards.
Once you understand this, you need to know that for AI architects, selecting the puzzle pieces correctly makes the result improve dramatically. In other words, the AI responds with coherent information and, of course, efficiently. There is a balance here. Plus, every month, these softwares evolve, and you have to update them or even replace pieces because a new one from another company might be better than the previous.
I’ll try to explain simply what happens in these 4 steps, and another day I’ll cover the subsequent steps.
1.
If you’ve heard of OCR (Optical Character Recognition) from the old days—software that scanned documents on a desktop scanner and converted what was on a sheet of paper into text in a Word or txt file—well, this is very similar. You pass the document, and the PARSER extracts all the data as best as possible. The complexity here comes from images, tables, formulas… And always keeping in mind there are two types of PDFs: those that are images where text can’t be selected, and those with selectable text (the latter are more recommended).
2.
Chunking or chunking. Once the document is scanned, now you have to chunk it so it can be stored and later searched. We’re preparing here for indexing, to later reference these chunks and display the information. You need to consider chunk size (by words, paragraphs, sentences…) and, of course, chunking speed.
3.
Once we have those chunks, we do the embedding, which is nothing more than replacing the text with matrices of numbers that will ultimately be called VECTORS.
4.
These vectors are what get stored in vector databases, and if you open them, they’ll be full of numerical matrices.
Now that you know what’s inside those vector databases, I’ll explain why. Then, later, someone searches for something in ChatGPT (5) That question gets chunked (6) to later be converted into a numerical vector (7) and searched in the vector database. In other words, it goes looking for the vector that most closely matches the one stored earlier in step 4. Like searching for a book in a library and, within the book itself, a chunk of text. The database returns that found vector (8), converts those numbers (9) back to text, and delivers all the information or related chunks from that search (10), and the AI puts all those chunks together (because it knows how to speak our language in the most coherent response possible (11)).
Well, that’s basically what happens behind ChatGPT every time you ask it something, and that’s why it needs powerful graphics cards. But on top of that, now there are companies specializing in each of those steps, and orchestrating them properly makes everything more efficient. There are even benchmarks for these steps, of course. Of course, in this whole scenario and in each of these steps, there are paid service versions and versions you can install locally. If you want to install everything locally (for privacy reasons), you’ll need to choose all open source options and, of course, have hardware that can process it quickly (a powerful GPU or graphics card). Currently (March 2024), the computers with the most powerful processors are Macs with their M2 and M3 chips. And the most powerful graphics cards are NVIDIA’s. A powerful graphics card costs over €1500.
